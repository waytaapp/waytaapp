Your home is set down for sale in execution. You have more room than the papers suggest.

Rule 46A of the Uniform Rules of Court requires a court to apply judicial oversight before a primary residence is sold in execution — including considering alternatives and, in most cases, setting a reserve price. That oversight is only useful if someone puts your circumstances in front of the court, in time.

We do not buy houses. Nothing on this page is an offer to purchase your property, and we will never make one. We are a consumer credit consultancy. We work only for homeowners — never for banks or credit providers.

Where you are in the process

The earlier you act, the more options remain open. Each stage below narrows them:

  • Section 129 letter — the notice a credit provider must deliver under the National Credit Act before it may litigate. The widest range of options exists here.
  • Summons issued — court proceedings have started. There is still time to oppose, to raise NCA defences, or to propose an arrangement.
  • Rule 46A application — the court is being asked to declare your home specially executable. This is the hearing where alternatives and a reserve price are argued.
  • Sale date set — narrow, but not always closed. Applications to rescind or to stay a sale are possible on the right facts.
